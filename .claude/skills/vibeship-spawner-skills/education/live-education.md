## Cohort-Based Course Design

### Why Cohorts Win
| Factor | Self-Paced | Cohort |
|--------|------------|--------|
| Completion | 5-15% | 60-85% |
| Accountability | None | Peers + deadlines |
| Networking | None | Built-in |
| Price point | $50-500 | $500-5000+ |

### Cohort Structure
```
Week 0: Orientation + community
Week 1-N: Core content + live sessions
Final Week: Capstone + celebration
Post-cohort: Alumni community
```

### Weekly Rhythm
| Day | Activity |
|-----|----------|
| Monday | New module unlocks |
| Tue-Thu | Async work + community |
| Friday | Live session |
| Weekend | Catch up, project work |

### Live Session Format (90 min)
1. Check-in / wins (10 min)
2. Hot seat / Q&A (30 min)
3. Teaching moment (20 min)
4. Breakout exercise (20 min)
5. Commitments for next week (10 min)

### Cohort Size Guidelines
| Size | Vibe | Price |
|------|------|-------|
| 10-20 | Intimate, high-touch | Premium |
| 20-50 | Balanced | Mid-tier |
| 50-100 | Community energy | Lower per-seat |
| 100+ | Event feel | Lowest per-seat |

## Workshop Facilitation

### Workshop vs Course
- Workshop: Single session, specific outcome
- Course: Multiple sessions, broader transformation

### Workshop Structure (2-3 hours)
| Segment | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Opening | 10 min | Energy, expectations |
| Teaching | 20 min | Core concept |
| Exercise 1 | 30 min | Apply learning |
| Break | 10 min | Reset energy |
| Teaching 2 | 15 min | Deeper layer |
| Exercise 2 | 30 min | Build on first |
| Share out | 20 min | Learn from peers |
| Close | 15 min | Next steps, resources |

### Energy Management
```
High energy → Teaching → Lower (absorbing)
→ Exercise (active) → Share (high) → Close
```

### Facilitation Techniques
- Chat waterfall: "Type your answer, hit enter on 3"
- Breakout rooms: 3-4 people, clear prompt
- Polls: Keep engagement, gather data
- Spotlight: Bring participants on camera

### The 10-Minute Rule
- Never talk for more than 10 minutes
- Always follow with interaction
- If > 10 min, insert micro-engagement

## Peer Learning Systems

### Why Peer Learning
- Students learn by teaching
- Diverse perspectives
- Scales instructor time
- Builds relationships

### Peer Learning Structures
| Structure | How It Works |
|-----------|--------------|
| Buddy pairs | 2 people, weekly check-ins |
| Pods | 4-6 people, shared projects |
| Peer review | Exchange and critique work |
| Hot seats | One person, group feedback |
| Mastermind | Rotating focus person |

### Buddy System
- Match in Week 1
- Weekly 30-min calls
- Accountability partner
- Progress check-ins

### Pod Design
- 4-6 people per pod
- Mix skill levels slightly
- Assign pod leader
- Shared Slack/Discord channel
- Weekly pod meetings

### Peer Review Process
1. Submit work by deadline
2. Randomly assign 2 reviewers
3. Provide structured feedback
4. Review reviews (quality control)

## Accountability Architecture

### Accountability Layers
| Layer | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------|
| Self | Clear deadlines, streaks |
| Peer | Buddy, pod, public commits |
| Instructor | Check-ins, progress tracking |
| Stakes | Deposit, social pressure |

### Public Commitment
- Week 1: Share your goal publicly
- Weekly: Share progress in community
- Final: Present to the group

### Progress Visibility
- Leaderboards (optional, can demotivate)
- Completion badges
- Progress bar for cohort
- Celebration of milestones

### Deadline Design
| Type | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Soft | "Aim for Friday" | Low accountability |
| Firm | "Due Friday" | Medium |
| Hard | "Live review Saturday" | High |

### Recovery Paths
- Don't punish falling behind
- Office hours for catch-up
- "Life happens" extensions
- Focus on finishing, not perfection

## Anti-Patterns

### Lecture-Only Live Sessions
Using live time for one-way content
**Why it's bad:** Wastes live format.
Could be async video.
Boring, passive.
No peer benefit.


### No Structure for Peer Connection
Expecting organic peer relationships
**Why it's bad:** Connections don't form.
Students feel isolated.
Missed cohort value.


### Too Many Live Sessions
Overwhelming calendar with calls
**Why it's bad:** Attendance drops.
"Call fatigue" sets in.
Feels like a job.

### [HIGH] Students stop showing up to live sessions

**Situation:** Attendance drops after first few sessions

**Why it happens:**
Sessions not valuable enough.
Wrong time for audience.
Life gets in the way.
Content could be async.


**Solution:**
```
## Fixing Low Attendance

### Make Sessions Unmissable
- Don't repeat recorded content
- Hot seats / live coaching
- Guest experts
- Peer activities
- Real-time feedback on their work

### Optimal Scheduling
| Audience | Best Times |
|----------|------------|
| US professionals | Tue-Thu 12pm or 7pm ET |
| Global | Rotate times or offer 2 |
| Parents | After 8pm or during school |

### Attendance Boosters
- Calendar invites with reminders
- Day-before email
- Morning-of Slack ping
- Attendance tracking visible
- Bonus for perfect attendance

### If They Miss
- Recordings available (but not too convenient)
- Buddy shares notes
- Quick catch-up option
- Don't guilt-trip

```

**Symptoms:**
- Attendance drops week over week
- Same people always missing
- Low energy in sessions
- Students watching recordings instead

### [MEDIUM] Breakout rooms are awkward and unproductive

**Situation:** Breakout sessions fail to generate engagement

**Why it happens:**
Unclear instructions.
No designated leader.
Wrong group size.
No accountability to share back.


**Solution:**
```
## Effective Breakout Rooms

### Setup Rules
- 3-4 people max per room
- Written instructions on screen
- Designate a "reporter"
- Time-box clearly (with warning)

### Instruction Template
```
In your breakout (10 min):
1. Each person shares X (2 min each)
2. Pick one to share with main room
3. Elect a reporter to present
```

### Facilitation
- Pop into rooms to check
- Broadcast time warnings
- Have backup plan for stragglers
- Celebrate share-outs

### If Still Struggling
- Start with pairs, then groups
- Use collaborative docs
- Give example of "good" response
- Make it about their real work

```

**Symptoms:**
- Silent breakout rooms
- We didn't know what to do
- Same people always talking
- Nothing to share back

### [HIGH] Running cohorts is exhausting and unsustainable

**Situation:** Instructor burns out after a few cohorts

**Why it happens:**
Too much live time.
Answering same questions.
No team support.
Emotional labor.


**Solution:**
```
## Sustainable Cohort Operations

### Time Boundaries
| Activity | Limit |
|----------|-------|
| Live sessions | 2-4 hrs/week |
| Community | 30 min/day |
| 1:1 coaching | Cap or eliminate |

### Leverage Others
- Guest instructors
- TA/community manager
- Peer support structures
- Alumni mentors

### Systematize Repetition
- FAQ document
- Recorded answers to common Qs
- Templates for feedback
- Standard operating procedures

### Recovery Between Cohorts
- 2-4 week break between
- Cohort retro and improvement
- Batching cohorts (2x/year vs monthly)

```

**Symptoms:**
- Dreading live sessions
- Resentment building
- Quality dropping
- Considering quitting teaching

### [MEDIUM] Global audience can't attend live sessions

**Situation:** Major portions of audience excluded by timing

**Why it happens:**
Single timezone focus.
Didn't consider global reach.
Can't please everyone.


**Solution:**
```
## Timezone-Friendly Design

### Options
| Approach | Trade-off |
|----------|-----------|
| Rotate times | Everyone inconvenienced equally |
| Two sessions | More instructor time |
| Regional cohorts | Smaller groups |
| Async-first | Loses some live magic |

### Async-Heavy Model
- 80% async content
- 1 live session/week (rotates)
- Recorded and shared quickly
- Community for always-on support

### If Keeping Single Time
- Be explicit in marketing
- Show timezone clearly
- Offer recording + notes
- Create regional peer groups

```

**Symptoms:**
- Complaints about timing
- Large groups never attending
- Refund requests citing timezone
- Low engagement from regions

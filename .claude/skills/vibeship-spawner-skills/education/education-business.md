## Course Pricing Framework

### Price Based on Transformation, Not Content
| Wrong Thinking | Right Thinking |
|----------------|----------------|
| "It's 10 hours of video" | "They'll land a $20K raise" |
| "Similar courses are $X" | "What's the outcome worth?" |
| "I'm not famous" | "Can I deliver the result?" |

### Pricing Tiers by Format
| Format | Typical Range | Justification |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Mini-course | $50-200 | Quick win, low commitment |
| Self-paced | $200-1000 | Complete transformation |
| Cohort | $500-3000 | Accountability + community |
| Premium cohort | $3000-10000 | High-touch, guaranteed results |
| Coaching + course | $5000-25000 | Done-with-you |

### Value-Based Pricing Formula
```
Price = (Outcome Value × Probability of Success) ÷ 10
```
Example: $50K salary increase × 30% success rate = $15K
$15K ÷ 10 = $1,500 feels fair

### Price Anchoring
- Show the DIY cost (time, mistakes)
- Compare to alternatives (bootcamp, degree)
- Frame against outcome value

### Discounting Rules
- Launch discount: 20-30% max
- Never more than 50%
- Increase price, don't lower it

## Educator Positioning

### The Positioning Stack
1. Topic (what you teach)
2. Audience (who you teach)
3. Method (how you teach)
4. Philosophy (why you teach it this way)

### Niche Down Formula
```
[Skill] for [Specific Audience] using [Unique Method]
```
- ❌ "Marketing course"
- ✅ "LinkedIn content for B2B consultants using the 3-2-1 framework"

### Authority Building
| Activity | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| Free content | Reach, proof of knowledge |
| Guest teaching | Borrowed authority |
| Case studies | Proof of results |
| Book/podcast | Credibility markers |

### The "Only" Positioning
What are you the only one who:
- Teaches [topic] to [audience]?
- Uses [method] for [outcome]?
- Has [unique background/experience]?

### Anti-Positioning
Define what you're NOT:
- "This isn't a get-rich-quick course"
- "Not for beginners / Not for experts"
- "No fluff, no theory - only practical"

## Anti-Patterns

### Underpricing Based on Imposter Syndrome
Pricing low because you don't feel qualified
**Why it's bad:** Attracts wrong students.
Unsustainable business.
Signals low value.
Burnout from volume needed.


### Launching to Nobody
Building course before building audience
**Why it's bad:** No one to launch to.
No validation of demand.
Crickets on launch day.


### Perpetual Launch Mode
Always launching, never optimizing
**Why it's bad:** Exhausting.
Revenue spikes and drops.
No compounding.

### [HIGH] Course launch generates minimal sales

**Situation:** Launch week ends with disappointing revenue

**Why it happens:**
Audience too small.
Positioning unclear.
Insufficient launch runway.
Price mismatch.


**Solution:**
```
## Recovering from a Launch Flop

### Immediate Assessment
| Metric | What It Tells You |
|--------|-------------------|
| Email opens | Audience engagement |
| Sales page visits | Interest level |
| Cart abandons | Pricing/offer issues |
| Actual sales | Market-message fit |

### Common Causes + Fixes
| Cause | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Small list | Grow list 3-6 months |
| Cold audience | Nurture before next launch |
| Unclear value | Rewrite positioning |
| Too expensive | Validate or lower price |
| Poor timing | Research audience calendar |

### Short-Term Recovery
- Extend cart (don't panic discount)
- Direct outreach to warm leads
- Offer payment plan
- Ask non-buyers why

### Long-Term Fix
- Build email list intentionally
- Warm up 4-6 weeks before launch
- Validate with pre-sales next time
- Get testimonials from small first cohort

```

**Symptoms:**
- Single-digit sales
- Low email engagement
- Sales page bounces
- Refund requests

### [HIGH] High refund rate after launch

**Situation:** Significant percentage request refunds

**Why it happens:**
Overpromised in marketing.
Course didn't match expectations.
Wrong audience attracted.
Buyer's remorse + easy refund.


**Solution:**
```
## Managing Refund Waves

### Healthy Refund Rates
| Price Point | Acceptable Rate |
|-------------|-----------------|
| < $200 | 5-10% |
| $200-1000 | 3-5% |
| > $1000 | 1-3% |

### Diagnosis
- Survey refund requesters
- Look for patterns in reasons
- Compare to marketing claims
- Check sales page expectations

### Common Causes
| Reason | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| "Not what I expected" | Clearer sales page |
| "Too basic/advanced" | Better targeting |
| "No time" | Pre-qualify commitment |
| "Didn't deliver promise" | Fix course or marketing |

### Prevention
- Show clear curriculum
- Include "who this is NOT for"
- Set realistic expectations
- Deliver value fast (reduce remorse window)

```

**Symptoms:**
- Refund requests spike
- Complaints about mismatch
- Negative reviews mention expectations
- Not what I thought

### [MEDIUM] Lowering price repeatedly, devaluing course

**Situation:** Discounting more and more to get sales

**Why it happens:**
Panic after slow sales.
Competing on price.
Imposter syndrome.
Not addressing real issue.


**Solution:**
```
## Escaping the Pricing Spiral

### Signs You're Spiraling
- Regular deep discounts
- "Sale" is permanent state
- Price dropped multiple times
- Competitors undercutting

### The Problem with Low Price
- Attracts non-committed students
- Lower completion rates
- Harder to invest in quality
- Can't afford to support students

### Recovery Path
1. Stop discounting (cold turkey)
2. Add value instead (bonuses)
3. Create premium tier
4. Reposition, don't re-price
5. Gather testimonials at any price

### If Sales Are Slow
| Instead of... | Do this... |
|---------------|------------|
| Lowering price | Add bonuses |
| Permanent sale | Limited-time offer |
| Competing on price | Differentiate on outcome |

```

**Symptoms:**
- Price lower than last launch
- Always on sale
- Justifying with "it's accessible"
- Competitors' prices driving yours

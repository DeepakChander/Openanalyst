```
## Positioning Framework

### 1. The Authority Triangle

```
      Expertise
        /\
       /  \
      /    \
     /______\
Passion    Market Need

Sweet spot: Where all three intersect
```

**Questions to Answer**
- Expertise: What do you know deeply that others don't?
- Passion: What would you write/speak about for free?
- Market Need: What does your audience need to hear?

### 2. Unique Angle Discovery

**The "Only" Test**
"I am the only person who [unique combination]"

Examples:
- "Only former banker turned developer building fintech"
- "Only enterprise seller who writes about PLG"
- "Only VC who codes production systems"

**Contrarian Positioning**
| Conventional Wisdom | Your Contrarian View |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| "Move fast and break things" | "Move deliberately, build to last" |
| "Raise as much as you can" | "Raise only what you need" |
| "Scale before profit" | "Profit enables sustainable scale" |

### 3. Topic Ownership

**Claim a Space**
```
Broad category → Your specific lens

"Leadership" → "Leadership for remote-first startups"
"AI" → "AI ethics for regulated industries"
"Sales" → "Consultative selling for technical products"
```

### 4. Voice Development

| Element | Questions |
|---------|-----------|
| Tone | Formal or casual? Academic or practical? |
| Stance | Provocative or balanced? Bold or measured? |
| Format | Stories or frameworks? Data or anecdotes? |
| Audience | Peers or aspirants? Beginners or experts? |

### 5. Positioning Statement

```
"I help [audience] understand [topic] through [unique lens],
based on my experience [credibility]."

Example:
"I help technical founders understand go-to-market through
a developer's lens, based on my experience building and
selling developer tools at [companies]."
```

## Content Strategy for Authority

### 1. Content Pillars

```
Define 3-5 pillars you'll own:

Pillar 1: Core expertise (deepest knowledge)
Pillar 2: Adjacent expertise (related credibility)
Pillar 3: Contrarian takes (unique perspectives)
Pillar 4: Industry commentary (current events)
Pillar 5: Personal journey (human connection)
```

### 2. Content Types for Authority

| Type | Purpose | Frequency |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Deep dives | Demonstrate expertise | Monthly |
| Frameworks | Create shareable tools | Quarterly |
| Hot takes | Show perspective | Weekly |
| Predictions | Build forward authority | Quarterly |
| Case studies | Prove results | As available |
| Personal lessons | Build trust | Monthly |

### 3. The Pillar-to-Pieces Model

```
Pillar Content (Long-form, high-effort)
├── LinkedIn post summarizing key point
├── Twitter thread breaking down framework
├── Newsletter section
├── Speaking topic
└── Quote graphics

1 pillar piece → 10+ derivative pieces
```

### 4. Controversial-Credible Balance

```
Too safe → Ignored
Too contrarian → Not credible

Sweet spot: "Surprising but true"
- Backed by evidence
- Against conventional wisdom
- But defensible when challenged
```

### 5. Citation Strategy

**Build citability:**
- Create named frameworks
- Conduct original research
- Coin terms for concepts
- Make bold, specific predictions

**Goal: Others quote you**

### 6. Consistency Formula

```
Weekly minimum:
- 1 long-form piece (blog, newsletter)
- 3-5 short-form posts
- 2-3 engagement responses

Monthly:
- 1 major piece
- 1 speaking/guest opportunity
- 1 collaboration

Quarterly:
- 1 signature piece (research, major framework)
```

**Variant Talks**

**Emerging Talks**

- Less polished

### 4. CFP (Call for Papers) Strategy
```

```
**Increase Odds**

- Multiple proposals

- Unique angle for their audience
```

```
## Earning Thought Leadership

### Signs of Earned Authority

- Cited in industry content
- Asked for quotes/opinions
- Invited to speak (not applied)

**Evidence of Impact**

```
Stage 1: Practitioner

### [HIGH] Publishing generic advice

**Situation:** Content is forgettable and interchangeable

**Why it happens:**
Generic content has no value.
Anyone could have said it.

**Solution:**
```
## Creating Non-Generic Content

### The Substitution Test

```
If you could put anyone else's name on this content
and it would still work, it's not thought leadership.

Good: "When I was at [company], we discovered..."
Bad: "Communication is important for teams"
```

### Non-Generic Content Types

**Experience-Based**
- What you learned from failure
- What surprised you
- What you'd do differently

**Contrarian**
- What you disagree with
- Why common advice is wrong
- Unpopular but true

**Novel Framework**
- New way to think about problem
- Named methodology
- Unique synthesis

**Specific Data**
- Your research
- Your numbers
- Your observations

### Platitude Detection

| Platitude | Add Specificity |
|-----------|-----------------|
| "Hire great people" | "Here's my exact interview process" |
| "Focus on customers" | "We talk to 5 customers/week using..." |
| "Move fast" | "We ship daily because of this process" |
| "Culture matters" | "We fired our top performer because..." |

### The "So What" Test

After every statement, ask "So what?"
If you can't answer with specific implication, cut it.

```

**Symptoms:**
- Content could be said by anyone
- Low engagement
- Nothing memorable

### [MEDIUM] Never admitting uncertainty

**Situation:** Appears arrogant or out of touch

**Why it happens:**
Nobody knows everything.
False confidence erodes trust.
Leaves no room to learn publicly.


**Solution:**
```
## Confident Humility

### Balance Formula

```
Strong opinions + Openness to new data

"Based on my experience, I believe X.
 Here's my evidence.
 I could be wrong if Y."
```

### Signaling Expertise + Humility

**Claim Expertise On**
- What you've done
- Data you've seen
- Decisions you've made

**Acknowledge Limits On**
- What's outside experience
- Where data is limited
- Rapidly changing areas

### Humility Phrases

| Arrogant | Humble but Confident |
|----------|----------------------|
| "The answer is X" | "In my experience, X" |
| "You should always" | "What's worked for me" |
| "Wrong approach" | "I'd consider instead" |
| "Obviously" | "What I've found" |

### Learning in Public

```
Share:
- What you got wrong
- How your views evolved
- What you're still figuring out

This builds more trust than perfection.
```

```

**Symptoms:**
- Seen as arrogant
- Pushback in comments

### [MEDIUM] Unsustainable content pace

**Situation:** Exhausted by content creation

**Why it happens:**
Started too ambitiously.
Not sustainable long-term.
Quality suffers.


**Solution:**
```
## Sustainable Thought Leadership

### Pace Calculation

```
Sustainable pace = (Hours available / Hours per piece)

Example:
- 4 hours/week for content
- 2 hours per short piece
- Sustainable: 2 pieces/week

Don't plan for more.
```

### Sustainability Strategies

**1. Batch Creation**
- Dedicate blocks of time
- Create multiple pieces at once
- Reduces context switching

**2. Repurposing**
- 1 long piece → multiple short pieces
- Same idea, different formats
- Leverage existing work

**3. Curation**
- Share others' work with commentary
- Less creation, still value
- Builds relationships

**4. Collaboration**
- Guest posts
- Interviews
- Co-creation

### Burnout Prevention

| Warning Sign | Response |
|--------------|----------|
| Dreading creation | Reduce frequency |
| Quality declining | Take a break |
| No new ideas | Consume more, create less |
| Feeling performative | Reconnect with why |

### Minimum Viable Consistency

```
Better than stopping:
- Reduce frequency
- Shorter content
- Curation mode
- Guest content

Consistency > volume
```

```

**Symptoms:**
- Falling behind on content
- Quality declining
- Resentment toward creating

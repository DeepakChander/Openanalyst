```
## Launch Story Structure

### 1. The Three-Act Launch

```
Act 1: Build Anticipation (Pre-Launch)
- Tease the problem
- Hint at solution
- Build waitlist/interest

Act 2: The Reveal (Launch)
- Dramatic announcement
- Full story revealed
- Call to action

Act 3: Sustain Momentum (Post-Launch)
- Customer stories
- Deeper dives
- Iteration updates
```

### 2. Launch Narrative Elements

| Element | Purpose | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Problem statement | Why this matters | "X has been broken for years" |
| Vision | The future state | "Imagine if..." |
| Solution | What you built | "Today, we're launching..." |
| Proof | Why it works | "Early users saw..." |
| Call to action | What to do now | "Try it free today" |

### 3. Story Angles

**Problem-Solution**
"X was broken. We fixed it."

**Mission-Driven**
"We believe [mission]. Today we take the next step."

**Evolution**
"From X to Y: Our journey to Z."

**Underdog**
"They said it couldn't be done. We did it anyway."

**Collaboration**
"Built with our community: What you asked for."

### 4. The Launch Hook

```
First sentence must grab attention:

"Today, everything changes for [audience]."
"After 2 years in stealth, we're ready to share..."
"This is the product we wish existed when..."
"What happens when you give [people] [power]?"
```

### 5. Launch Asset Framework

| Asset | Purpose | Length |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Launch video | Emotional hook | 60-90 sec |
| Blog post | Full story | 1000-2000 words |
| Press release | Media pickup | 500-700 words |
| Tweet thread | Social sharing | 5-10 tweets |
| Demo | Show the product | 3-5 min |
```

```
## Timing Your Launch

### 1. Day of Week Impact

| Day | Pros | Cons |
|-----|------|------|
| Monday | Fresh week, high energy | Competing with weekend backlog |
| Tuesday | Best for B2B | None significant |
| Wednesday | Good engagement | Mid-week noise |
| Thursday | Good for PR | Friday cliff |
| Friday | Fun, casual launches | Weekend drops off |

**Best for most launches: Tuesday or Thursday**

### 2. Time of Day

```
B2B launches: 9-10am PT (catches both coasts)
Consumer: 7-9am PT (before work browsing)
Global: Multiple announcements by region
Product Hunt: Midnight PT (for 24hr ranking)
```

### 3. Seasonal Considerations

| Period | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| January | New year energy, budget cycles |
| Summer | Lower engagement, vacation |
| September | Back to work, attention |
| Q4 | Holiday noise, budget spend |
| December | Year-end reviews, low attention |

### 4. Competition Awareness

**Avoid launching during:**
- Major competitor announcements
- Industry flagship events (unless presenting)
- Major tech company events (Apple, Google I/O)
- Major news events

**Consider launching during:**
- Industry events where you're present
- Relevant awareness weeks/days
- Complementary company announcements

### 5. Internal Readiness

```
Launch when:
✓ Product is stable
✓ Support is prepared
✓ Sales is trained
✓ Marketing assets ready
✓ PR is coordinated

Don't launch when:
- Key people unavailable
- Major bugs known
- Support understaffed
- Assets incomplete

## Anti-Patterns

### Feature List Launch
Launching with feature list instead of story
**Why it's bad:** Features don't resonate emotionally.
Forgettable.
Competitors can match features.


### Premature Launch
Launching before ready
**Why it's bad:** Bad first impressions stick.
Wastes launch moment.
Hard to re-launch.


### Launch and Forget
No post-launch plan
**Why it's bad:** Momentum dies immediately.
Misses follow-up opportunities.
Wasted launch effort.


### Single Channel Launch
Launching on one channel only
**Why it's bad:** Limited reach.
Misses audience segments.
Doesn't create "everywhere" perception.


### No Metrics Definition
Launching without success criteria
**Why it's bad:** Can't evaluate success.
No learning for future.
Can't prove ROI.

```
## Managing Expectations

### The Hype-Reality Gap

```
Hype Level → Expectation → Reality → Reaction

Over-hype: High → Very High → Medium → Disappointment
Right-size: High → High → High → Satisfaction
Under-sell: Medium → Medium → High → Delight
```

### Expectation Setting

**Do This**
- Be specific about what it does
- Show real product, not mockups
- Include limitations honestly
- Let product speak for itself

**Don't Do This**
- Superlatives without proof ("revolutionary")
- Vague promises ("changes everything")
- Hide limitations
- Over-produced visuals

### Promise-Proof Alignment

| You Promise | You Prove |
|-------------|-----------|
| "Fastest" | Speed benchmarks |
| "Easiest" | User test videos |
| "Best" | Comparison data |
| "First" | No competitors do X |

If you can't prove it, don't promise it.

### Early Access Reality Check

```
Before launch, have 10 users try it:
- Did they understand it?
- Did it work as promised?
- What confused them?
- Does experience match messaging?
```

### Recovery from Overhype

1. Acknowledge gap honestly
2. Share what you're fixing
3. Show quick improvements
4. Rebuild trust gradually

```

**Symptoms:**
- Negative comments after launch
- This isn't what I expected
- Refund requests

```
## Minimum Viable Launch

### Even "Quiet" Launches Need

```
Absolute minimum:
- Blog post announcing
- Email to existing users/list
- Social media posts
- Update to website

Takes: 4-8 hours
Impact: 10x vs no launch
```

### When Quiet is Right

**Intentionally quiet for:**
- Early beta (want feedback, not traffic)
- Sensitive features (rolling out carefully)
- B2B enterprise (private announcements)
- Testing messaging

**Not quiet because:**
- No time/resources
- Don't know how
- Assume it's not important

### Staged Launch Approach

```
Week 1: Soft launch (limited audience)
Week 2: Community launch (core users)
Week 3: Public launch (full push)

Each stage builds on previous learnings
```

### Post-Launch Amplification

If launch was too quiet:
- Re-launch with different angle
- "What you might have missed"
- Customer story as launch content
- Product Hunt or community launch

You can launch the same thing multiple times.
```

```
## Internal Launch Readiness

### Readiness Checklist

```
Product:
[ ] Feature complete
[ ] Major bugs fixed
[ ] Documentation ready
[ ] Performance tested

Support:
[ ] Team trained on product
[ ] FAQ prepared
[ ] Ticket system ready
[ ] Escalation path clear

Sales:
[ ] Sales team briefed
[ ] Materials ready
[ ] Pricing finalized
[ ] Demo environment ready

Operations:
[ ] Billing works
[ ] Onboarding flow tested
[ ] Monitoring in place
[ ] Scaling plan if needed
```

### Soft Launch Benefits

```
Limited launch first:
- Test support load
- Find operational gaps
- Train team on real issues
- Build internal confidence
```

### Launch Capacity Planning

**Estimate Load**
- Expected signups
- Support tickets per signup
- Sales inquiries expected
- System load expected

**Staff Accordingly**
- Extra support coverage launch week
- All-hands on deck day 1
- Clear escalation paths
- War room for quick decisions

| Timeframe | Support Mode |
|-----------|--------------|

| Days 2-3 | Elevated staffing |
```
